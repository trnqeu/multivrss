Infinite scroll was invented to keep you on the page longer. That's it. The metric it optimizes is not your comprehension, not your satisfaction, not the quality of what you read — it's time-on-site.

## The problem with endless

When there is no bottom, there is no done. You can't finish reading your feed. You can only stop, and stopping feels like giving up.

This is intentional. The absence of an endpoint removes the natural moment where you'd close the tab and do something else.

## What we do instead

MultivRSS paginates. You see a fixed set of items. You read them — or you don't — and then there is a next page button. A button that you choose to press.

That small friction is the point. It puts the decision back in your hands.

## The tradeoff

We know pagination feels slower at first. You have to click. You have to wait a beat. Some items you'd have scrolled past in two seconds now take an interaction.

We think that's fine. If an item isn't worth a scroll, it probably wasn't worth reading either.

The goal of MultivRSS is not to maximize the number of articles you see. It's to make the ones you do see count.
