Google Reader shut down in 2013. Pocket was acquired, then neutered. Instapaper went dark for European users. Readability disappeared overnight.

If you've been saving articles for more than a few years, you've lost them at least once.

## The problem with hosted reading lists

When you save an article to a proprietary service, you're not saving the article. You're saving a pointer that the service controls. When the service closes, the pointers disappear.

Your reading history is not a feature. It's data. And data should belong to you.

## What open format means

MultivRSS stores your saved links as plain records in a database you can export at any time. No proprietary format. No vendor lock-in. A CSV export is three clicks away.

We also expose a read API so you can pull your reading list into any tool that speaks JSON.

## The deeper point

Reading is a long game. The articles you save today you might read in five years — or return to in ten. A reading list that depends on a startup surviving is not a reading list. It's a bet.

Build your library on open formats. Export often. Own your history.
